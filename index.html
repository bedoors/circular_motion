Newton’s law of universal gravitation

Any two masses pull on each other. The force grows with each mass and falls off with the square of the distance between their centers.

Choose a pair

Size of each force:

F1 on 2 = F2 on 1 = G m1m2r²

Each mass pulls the other toward its center, and r is measured center to center. The two forces are a third-law pair, equal in size and opposite in direction: F1 on 2 = −F2 on 1.

G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg², the same everywhere in the universe. Double m1 or m2 and the force doubles. Double r and it drops to 14.

Circular orbits

Launch fast enough and a projectile falls around Earth instead of into it.

vorbit = g r

In orbit the only force is gravity, and it supplies exactly the centripetal acceleration v²r.

Kepler’s laws of planetary motion

Three rules describe how planets move around the Sun. Newton showed that all three follow from his law of universal gravitation.

1st law (orbits): every planet moves in an ellipse, with the Sun at one focus.

r1 + r2 = 2a

The distances to the two foci always add up to 2a, the long axis of the ellipse. A circle is the case e = 0.

Newton: a pull toward the Sun of F = G M mr² produces exactly these ellipses.

2nd law (areas): the line from the Sun to the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times.

So the planet moves fastest when it is closest to the Sun and slowest when it is farthest away.

Newton: gravity always points straight at the Sun, so it never changes how fast that area is swept.

3rd law (periods): the square of a planet’s period is proportional to the cube of its distance from the Sun.
